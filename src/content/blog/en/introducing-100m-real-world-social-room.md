We live in the most hyper-connected era in human history, yet we have never felt more isolated in public spaces.

Walk into any coffee shop, tech conference, coworking space, or music festival. You will see dozens of people sitting side by side, working on laptops or glancing at their phones. Many share common interests, work in the same industry, or want to meet new people. Yet, almost nobody talks. 

Breaking the ice with a stranger across the room feels awkward and intimidating. Meanwhile, traditional dating and proximity apps made the problem worse by introducing fake profiles, spam, and continuous battery-draining background tracking.

Today, we are launching [**100m** (100m.workouse.com)](https://100m.workouse.com)—a privacy-first mobile app that turns real-world physical venues into temporary, location-verified **social rooms**.

## 1. The Real-World Social Disconnect

Why is it so hard to start a spontaneous conversation in physical venues today?

| Dimension | Traditional Proximity Apps | The 100m Real-World Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Location Scope** | Profiles located miles away | Verified users sitting in the exact same room |
| **Privacy & GPS** | 24/7 invasive background tracking | Zero background tracking (one-time QR check) |
| **User Experience** | Endless swipe fatigue and spam DMs | Dual opt-in intent tags with zero unprompted DMs |
| **Outcome** | Distant digital chats that rarely meet | Immediate, face-to-face real-world connection |

### Why Existing Proximity Apps Failed

1. **Continuous Background Tracking**: Most location apps track your GPS 24/7 in the background. They drain your battery and upload your entire movement history to centralized databases. People rightly reject this surveillance.
2. **No Proof of Physical Presence**: Apps that show "people nearby" often include users who drove past 30 minutes ago or sit in a building half a mile away. Without physical co-presence, there is no shared context.
3. **Invasive Outreach & Harassment**: Opening an open inbox to everyone nearby invites unsolicited messages and unwanted attention. Users—especially women—quickly turn off location sharing to protect their peace.

## 2. What is 100m?

[**100m**](https://100m.workouse.com) is built around a single rule: **You can only see and interact with people who are physically in the exact same venue right now.**

When you leave the venue or your session expires, the room disappears. There is no background tracking, no remote browsing, and no persistent inbox.

### The 4-Step Interaction Cycle

- **Step 1: Scan & Verify**  
  Scan the dynamic QR code displayed at the venue counter or table. The app checks your GPS coordinates once to confirm you are within a ~100-meter radius of the venue.

- **Step 2: Join the Live Room**  
  Enter the ephemeral venue feed. See active check-ins, view intent badges (e.g. *"Looking to chat about AI"*, *"Open for coffee"*, *"Working solo"*), and read the venue wall.

- **Step 3: Send an Approach**  
  Notice someone interesting? Send a polite, structured "Approach" indicating your intent. Personal social profiles (like LinkedIn or Instagram) remain completely hidden.

- **Step 4: Dual Opt-In & Say Hello**  
  Only if the other person accepts your approach do your selected social handles unlock. You can now walk over, say hello, and break the ice with zero awkwardness.

## 3. Privacy-First Architecture

100m is engineered with strict data minimization, zero movement tracking, and sub-second edge response times:

- **No Continuous Tracking**: The app requests GPS permissions only during the active scan action. Coordinates are never written to disk or database logs.
- **60-Minute Automatic Expiration**: All check-ins expire automatically after 60 minutes. When you leave the venue, your presence in the room vanishes.
- **Mutual Consent by Default**: No user can message another without an explicit two-way acceptance. Users can block or flag unwanted interactions with one tap.
- **Edge-Native Speed**: Built on distributed edge infrastructure with sub-100ms response times globally.
